How to Bail on Friedsgiving After Buying GTA 6

You said you'd be there. Your friends said this was your last chance. Then you bought GTA 6. Here is the expert-approved, seven-step plan for getting out of Friedsgiving with whatever friendship remains.

MRBy Mason Respawn
November 20, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  Gaming Culture

There comes a moment in every gamer's life when two equally important commitments collide: the people who have stood by you for years, and the brand-new video game you purchased approximately 14 minutes ago.

For a growing number of players, that moment has arrived just in time for Friedsgiving — the sacred annual gathering of friends, fried food, and repeated warnings that you're officially on your last chance to show up.

Unfortunately, GTA 6 is now sitting in your library, and the calendar doesn't care that the entire Friedsgiving crew planned this weeks ago. While conventional wisdom suggests keeping your promises, our team consulted several entirely fictional specialists to develop a more gamer-friendly approach.

“Buying GTA 6 creates a temporary but medically unrecognized condition known as Acute Vice City Commitment Disorder. The symptoms include selective hearing, suspicious scheduling conflicts and the inability to put down a controller.”— Dr. Trevor Pending, fictional director of the Institute for Recreational Priorities

The official seven-step Friedsgiving exit protocol

Our experts stress that the key is preparation. A successful bail isn't just about not attending — it's about making your friends question whether attendance was ever a reasonable expectation in the first place.

01Phase One: Denial

Do not immediately announce you bought GTA 6.

For the first several hours, maintain the appearance of a normal, socially available adult. Continue liking messages in the Friedsgiving group chat. React positively to the menu. If someone asks whether you're excited, reply with something vague like, “Oh, absolutely.”

Expert tip: Do not specify which event you're excited about.

02Phase Two: Soft Launch

Begin expressing mild concerns about the weekend.

Twenty-four hours before Friedsgiving, casually mention that you've “got a lot going on.” This creates an emotional paper trail that suggests your eventual absence was the result of mounting circumstances rather than the delivery of a $90 video game.

Suggested message: “Everyone, this weekend is looking a little chaotic. Still hoping to make it though.”

The words “still hoping” are especially useful because they sound committed while promising absolutely nothing.

03Phase Three: The Pivot

Reveal the “unexpected situation.”

At this point, the truth must be introduced — carefully. Never open with “I'd rather play GTA.” Instead, explain that something you've waited a very long time for has finally become available and that the timing could not be worse.

“The phrase ‘I've waited years for this’ sounds like a deeply personal milestone. That's because it is. It just happens to have a wanted-level system.”— Professor Paige Disconnect, fictional chair of Applied Excuse Sciences
04Phase Four: Crisis Management

Acknowledge that this is, technically, your last chance.

If your friends have previously warned that another cancellation would result in permanent removal from the group chat, do not panic. This simply means they've reached the bargaining stage of the friendship lifecycle.

Deploy a statement such as, “I know how bad this looks,” followed by “I fully understand why you're upset.” These phrases are effective because they acknowledge the disaster without requiring you to leave your house.

05Phase Five: Restitution

Offer a peace treaty in the form of fried food.

Friedsgiving is fundamentally about friendship, but it's also about food. Offer to chip in for wings, fries, gravy or whatever dangerously delicious item is being served. Financial contribution does not legally constitute attendance, but it may reduce hostilities.

Suggested message: “I'm not saying I deserve forgiveness, but I'll cover the fries.”

Our experts classify this as reparations by appetizer, a technique with an entirely invented 87% success rate.

06Phase Six: Extraction

Send the final cancellation before the food is ordered.

Do not wait until everyone's sitting around wondering where you are. Deliver a concise announcement, accept that you'll be roasted in the group chat, and avoid inventing a fake medical emergency. Nobody needs that — especially when the actual emergency has a 100-hour story mode.

The recommended final message: “Friends, I regret to inform you that I will not be attending Friedsgiving. This decision was not made lightly. GTA 6 has entered my household, and I need to be there for it during this important transition. I understand the consequences. Please enjoy the chicken on my behalf.”
07Phase Seven: Aftercare

Return to society with a peace offering.

Once you've finished your first marathon gaming session, send a sincere apology and arrange a replacement hangout. You may still need to buy dinner. You may be temporarily demoted from “core friend group” to “person who sometimes reacts to the group chat.” These are acceptable losses.

Remember: GTA 6 will still be there tomorrow. So will your friends — probably.

The final verdict

Should you ditch Friedsgiving for GTA 6? According to literally every real friendship expert we did not contact, probably not. But if you're determined to make the worst possible decision, at least make it memorable.

And if someone forwards you this article in the group chat, understand that they are not asking for advice. They are giving you advance notice.
